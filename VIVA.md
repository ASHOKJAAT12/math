## Phase 17 Advanced Analysis Context

### Q: What is parameter sensitivity in numerical methods?
**A:** Parameter sensitivity refers to how drastically a method's output (error, execution iteration count, or convergence stability) changes when an input parameter (like initial guess, h, n, or tolerance) is slightly modified. 

### Q: Why does tolerance affect iterations in Root Finding?
**A:** Stricter (smaller) tolerances demand the root to be approximated to a higher decimal precision. Since numerical methods converge progressively per iteration, obtaining more precision mathematically requires passing through more iterations to narrow the residual boundary.

### Q: Why does h affect differentiation accuracy?
**A:** Because differentiation formulas are derived from Taylor series truncation, their mathematical accuracy (Truncation Error) is directly proportional to how small 'h' is. However, if 'h' gets too small natively in the computer, floating-point Round-Off error dominates and ruins the approximation. This is the optimal 'h' dilemma.

### Q: Why does n affect integration approximation?
**A:** Numerical integration partitions a curve into 'n' discrete sub-shapes (trapezoids or parabolas). A higher 'n' means the shapes are thinner and trace the highly-curved components of the function much more accurately, reducing overall Truncation error.

### Q: Why can numerical behavior vary between methods on the exact same problem?
**A:** Different methods use entirely different internal bounding assumptions (e.g., Secant uses secant lines based on two past points, whereas Newton uses exact tangent slopes with one point). A specific function's geometry (like being highly flat vs highly vertical) will benefit one geometric assumption over another.
